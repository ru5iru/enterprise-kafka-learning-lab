## 1. Goal and context

Review how concurrent tasks share memory before choosing pools and futures. Future Kafka processing must preserve ownership and ordering; threads alone do not provide either.

## 2. Concepts explained in my own words

Concurrency allows overlapping progress; parallelism runs work simultaneously. A race concerns a result dependent on interleaving; a data race has conflicting accesses without required ordering. Happens-before rules constrain visibility/order through mechanisms such as monitors, volatile communication, thread start/join and concurrent utilities.

A volatile field is not an atomic read-modify-write transaction: read, add and write can overlap. AtomicInteger increments address one counter; synchronized can protect a larger invariant when every related access follows the same lock. Thread confinement and immutable snapshots often avoid sharing altogether. A concurrent map still does not make arbitrary multi-step business logic atomic.

The exercise makes a lost update deterministic with latches, then contrasts atomic and monitor updates. It is not a visibility litmus test. Sleep does not establish the intended coordination contract. Locks need deliberate ordering and short critical sections; avoid external blocking calls while holding them. Java memory safety does not imply Kafka partition order or safe consumer sharing.

## 4. Decisions and rationale

Use latch coordination over a flaky stress loop or arbitrary sleep. The intentionally broken compound operation is isolated and documented; safe variants use AtomicInteger/one monitor. Prefer confinement for future designs where practical. No service threading policy/ADR introduced.

## 5. Best practices and design patterns

Applies happens-before reasoning, mutual exclusion, atomic update and explicit ownership. A deterministic bad interleaving teaches the failure directly. Actual Kafka worker/offset/rebalance patterns remain later tasks and require client-specific contracts.

## 6. Common mistakes, errors and vulnerabilities

Observed a lost update despite volatile; both safe counters completed. Deadlocks, unsafely published mutable data, blocking under locks and assuming concurrent-container compound safety are reviewed risks only. No claim that this finite test exhausts interleavings.

## 7. Testing, verification and evidence

## 8. Debugging and operational notes

When counters drift, identify the invariant and compound steps before adding volatile. Replace sleep coordination with explicit signals, inspect ownership and verify every access follows the chosen discipline. A failed wait must fail the probe, not silently lower expected counts.

## 9. Security and SSDLC check

Shared state and unbounded threads can affect integrity/availability. This exercise bounds threads, work and waits, uses synthetic counters and checks cleanup. No secrets or shared service state introduced. Root-default WSL remains inherited, not a concurrency guarantee.

## 10. Lessons learned and knowledge checks

Learned: visibility differs from atomicity; same-value reads can be coordinated without randomness; monitors can guard multi-step invariants; thread completion must be checked. Can explain why volatile counter++ is insufficient and why a concurrent map alone does not ensure idempotent processing. Next thread pools.
