Define just enough synthetic commerce behavior to drive Kafka publish/subscribe, duplicate, partial-failure, and later consistency experiments without building a real shop.

## 2. Concepts explained in my own words

The simulated domain supplies observable side effects. An accepted order and separately completed payment/inventory steps do not form a distributed transaction. Notifications acknowledge an individual observed outcome; they must not imply that every branch or a global order workflow has completed.

## 5. Best practices and design patterns

Use stable synthetic order/event identifiers, explicit scenario inputs, bounded repeatable failures, and event-to-effect evidence. Keep financial and inventory effects fake.

Publish/subscribe and eventual consistency are the planned message-flow patterns. Idempotency, Outbox, and Saga are learning targets introduced by their own later tasks, not asserted as baseline protection.

Implementation and production controls remain with their scheduled tasks; this is a planning result, not proof of runtime reliability or proficiency.

## 6. Common mistakes, errors and vulnerabilities

| Risk / failure mode | Trigger / example | Prevention / mitigation | Evidence and residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| False completion | Payment succeeds but inventory fails | Expose independent branch observations; no global success claim | Scenario review only; distributed flow not built |
| Unsafe realism | Send real email or charge money | Record local synthetic notification/payment effects | No external integration |
| Replay side effects | Same event reaches consumer twice | Include duplicate case and measure effects before later idempotency | No deduplication claimed before P6/P8 |

These are reviewed planning risks; no exploit, load test, or fault injection was executed.

## 10. Lessons learned and knowledge checks

- Kafka can decouple branches without making them atomic.
- A synthetic failure is more useful when its expected effect is deterministic.
- Repeated delivery and repeated business effects must be counted separately.
- An individual notification is not a global completion guarantee.

Remaining limitations: No schemas, API implementation, retry settings, database tables, topic names, or saga orchestrator are selected. P7 defines contracts and P8 evolves persistence/compensation. Negative outcome contracts require explicit task-level design before use.
